You are a senior NLP researcher who has published extensively at EMNLP, ACL, NAACL, and COLING and has served as an EMNLP Area Chair and reviewer.
Your task is to write the Related Work section of an EMNLP Main Track paper.
The objective is not to summarize papers one by one, but to critically position the proposed work within the existing literature, identify research gaps, and motivate the need for the proposed framework.

YOU HAVE TO GO THRPUGH THE WHOLE CODEBASE. 

Use only the provided references and information.
Never invent papers, citations, results, or claims. PLEASE TAKE AT LEAST 12 to 15 papers (You can take paper from introduction) 

Writing Style
Write in the style of accepted EMNLP, ACL, or NAACL papers.
The writing should be
technically precise
concise
logically connected
objective
critical rather than descriptive
Avoid
listing papers chronologically
describing each paper independently
excessive historical background
marketing language
unsupported claims
Assume readers are experienced NLP researchers.

Overall Organization
Organize the Related Work theme-wise, not paper-wise.
Each subsection should discuss one research direction.
Recommended structure:
2.1 Research Area A
depending on the paper.

2.2 Research Area B
Discuss another major line of work.
Compare methods.
Identify common assumptions.
Discuss strengths and weaknesses.

2.3 Research Area C
Continue with another closely related direction.
Explain how it differs from previous categories.

Writing Each Subsection
For every subsection, follow this structure.
Paragraph 1
Introduce the research direction.
Explain its goal.
Describe the common methodologies.
Summarize representative approaches by grouping similar works instead of describing them individually.

Paragraph 2
Discuss
strengths,
limitations,
unresolved challenges,
assumptions,
remaining research gaps.
Critically compare the existing approaches.
Do not criticize without evidence.

Closing Sentence
End every subsection by explaining
what limitation remains
and
why it motivates the proposed framework.
This sentence should naturally connect to your work.

Comparative Analysis
Instead of writing
"Paper A does..."
"Paper B does..."
"Paper C does..."
prefer writing
"Existing retrieval-based approaches primarily rely on ... whereas recent agent-based systems introduce ... However, both families of methods still struggle with ..."
Compare ideas, not individual papers.

Positioning the Proposed Work
At the end of the Related Work section, write one short synthesis paragraph.
This paragraph should
summarize the common limitation across prior work,
explain the research gap,
position the proposed framework,
avoid repeating the Introduction,
avoid describing experiments or results.
Do not claim superiority.
Simply explain how the proposed work addresses the identified gap.

Citations
When discussing prior work,
group related papers together,
cite representative works naturally,
avoid excessive citation density,
avoid listing many citations without discussion.
Every citation should support a meaningful statement.

Flow
Ensure the section follows this logical progression.
Research Direction A
↓
Research Direction B

Research Direction C

Please only give three research direction.

Quality Checklist
Before producing the final Related Work section, verify that
The section is organized theme-wise, not paper-wise.
Every subsection has a clear focus.
Similar papers are synthesized rather than individually summarized.
Strengths and limitations of prior work are critically discussed.
Every subsection ends by identifying an unresolved limitation.
The overall research gap emerges naturally.
The final paragraph clearly positions the proposed framework without overselling it.
No experimental results from the current paper appear in this section.
The writing matches the quality and style of accepted EMNLP Main Track papers.
The section reads as a coherent narrative rather than a collection of paper summaries.
If any criterion is not satisfied, revise the section before returning the final version.
Return only the completed Related Work section, using polished scientific writing with clear subsection headings.
